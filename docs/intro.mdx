# Introduction

Welcome to **Physical AI and Humanoid Robotics: Building Intelligent Machines**.

Physical AI is the field at the intersection of machine learning, robotics, and
control theory where software models stop living in the cloud and start living
in bodies — machines that sense, move, and act in the physical world. Humanoid
robots are the most demanding expression of that idea: an artificial body
roughly shaped like ours, walking on two legs, using two arms, and trying to
do the things we do.

This book is a practical, engineering-oriented guide to building them.

## What this book covers

- The **foundations** of physical AI: what makes embodied intelligence different
  from digital AI, and the math that ties sensing to action.
- **Perception**: how a robot sees, feels, and keeps track of where it is.
- **Actuation and control**: motors, gears, kinematics, and the control loops
  that keep a biped upright.
- **Learning and autonomy**: reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and the
  new vision–language–action models that give robots instructions-level autonomy.
- **Systems and deployment**: how the software, simulation, and safety stack
  come together in a working robot.

## Who this book is for

Working engineers, graduate students, and advanced hobbyists who already know
how to program and have some exposure to machine learning. We keep the math
explicit but practical — every idea is connected to hardware you can buy and
code you can run.

## How to read it

The book is organized into five parts plus appendices. You can read it straight
through, or jump to a specific part. Each chapter ends with a short set of
exercises. Code and configuration snippets assume the ecosystem around
**ROS 2**, **Python**, and the **MuJoCo / Isaac Lab** simulation stack, but the
core ideas transfer to any robot.
