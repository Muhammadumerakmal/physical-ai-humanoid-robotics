# About This Book

## The next decade of AI has a body

For seventy years, artificial intelligence lived behind glass. It played chess,
sorted photos, and — lately — writes essays and code. But it never had to catch a
falling glass, keep its balance on a slope, or decide how hard to grip an egg.
That era is ending. The frontier of AI is moving out of the data center and into
**bodies that sense, decide, and act in the real world**.

This is **Physical AI**: the place where machine learning, robotics, and control
theory meet. And its most demanding expression is the **humanoid** — a machine
shaped like us, walking on two legs, working with two hands, operating in a world
built for human bodies.

The catch is that embodiment is *hard* in ways digital AI never was. The physical
world is noisy, continuous, unforgiving, and runs in real time with no undo. A
model that is 99% accurate in a notebook can still fall flat on its face. Bridging
that gap — from clean code to a machine that stays upright — is a craft, and this
book teaches it from first principles to a working robot.

:::tip What makes this book different
It doesn't stop at theory. It ends on an interactive humanoid you command in
plain English, right in your browser — the perception, control, and learning
ideas from every chapter wired into one embodied loop. There's also a companion
**AI tutor**, grounded in the book itself, ready on every page.
:::

## What This Book Is

A single source of truth for the full stack of an intelligent machine. It covers:

- The **foundations** of physical AI: why embodied intelligence differs from
  digital AI, and the math that ties sensing to action.
- **Perception**: how a robot sees, feels, and keeps track of where it is.
- **Actuation and control**: motors, kinematics, and the control loops that keep
  a biped upright.
- **Learning and autonomy**: machine learning, reinforcement learning,
  imitation, and the vision-language-action models that give robots
  instructions-level autonomy.
- **Systems and deployment**: the software stack, ROS 2, simulation, and how a
  robot ships to the real world.

## Who This Book Is For

- **Beginner AI engineers** — you already know models and data; this book maps
  them onto bodies and physics.
- **Software developers entering robotics** — you already write code; this book
  adds the physical layer underneath it.
- **Students** — a structured path from first principles to a working humanoid,
  with exercises at the end of every chapter.

## What You'll Be Able to Do

By the end of the book you will be able to:

- Explain the full architecture of a physical-AI system, from sensors to actuators.
- Write and run robot code in **Python** and **ROS 2**.
- Set up a simulation, train a policy, and deploy it to hardware.
- Choose between perception, control, and learning approaches for a real task.
- Design and evaluate a simple robot system end to end.

## Find Your Path

The book works as a straight read, but you can also jump in:

- **Straight through** (recommended) — read Parts I–VI in order for the complete
  foundation.
- **Builder first** — hands-on from the start: skim the systems chapters early
  and circle back to foundations as questions arise.
- **Software fast entry** — go straight to the learning and systems parts, then
  fill in sensing and control.
- **Research focus** — start with the learning and future parts, then deepen
  into perception and control.

Every chapter ends with **exercises** that turn reading into building.

## How the Book Is Structured

Six parts plus appendices; each part is a layer of the stack:

- **Part I — Foundations**: what physical AI is, robotics foundations, the math
  toolkit, and humanoid architecture.
- **Part II — Sensing & Perception**: sensors and actuators, perception,
  computer vision, and state estimation.
- **Part III — Actuation & Control**: kinematics and dynamics, motion control,
  balance and locomotion, and manipulation.
- **Part IV — Learning & Intelligence**: machine learning, reinforcement
  learning, imitation, VLA models, LLMs, and AI agents.
- **Part V — Systems, Simulation & Deployment**: ROS 2, the software stack,
  simulation, Isaac Sim, and real-world deployment.
- **Part VI — The Road Ahead**: the future of humanoid robotics.
- **Appendices**: glossary, resources, environment setup, a math reference, and
  project ideas.

The recommended starting point is
**[Part I — Foundations](/docs/part1-foundations/what-is-physical-ai)**.
