# Math Quick Reference

Everything the chapters lean on, in one place, so you can look up a formula
without re-reading. This is a reference, not a tutorial — the derivations live in
Part I's *Math for Embodied Machines* and in the chapters that use each tool.

## Vectors & frames

A point in 3D is a column vector $\mathbf{p} = \begin{bmatrix} x & y & z \end{bmatrix}^\top$.
Robotics is largely bookkeeping of **frames**: the same point has different
coordinates in the world frame, the base frame, and the hand frame.

- Dot product (projection / angle): $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = \|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta$.
- Cross product (axis of rotation, torque): $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ is perpendicular to both.
- Norm: $\|\mathbf{a}\| = \sqrt{\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a}}$.

## Rotation matrices

A rotation is a matrix $R \in SO(3)$ with $R^\top R = I$ and $\det R = 1$. It maps
coordinates in one frame to another and **preserves lengths and angles**.

$$
R_z(\theta) =
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Key facts:

- Inverse = transpose: $R^{-1} = R^\top$.
- Compose by multiplication: $R_{AC} = R_{AB}\,R_{BC}$ (order matters).
- The columns of $R$ are the axes of the rotated frame expressed in the original.

## Quaternions

A unit quaternion $q = (w, x, y, z)$ with $\|q\| = 1$ represents a rotation
without gimbal lock and with cheap interpolation — which is why estimators and
animation use it instead of Euler angles.

- Identity (no rotation): $q = (1, 0, 0, 0)$.
- Compose rotations: quaternion product $q_1 \otimes q_2$.
- Inverse rotation (unit $q$): the conjugate $q^{*} = (w, -x, -y, -z)$.
- Smooth interpolation between orientations: **slerp**.

## Homogeneous transforms

Pose = rotation **and** translation, packed into one $4\times4$ matrix
$T \in SE(3)$ so that poses compose by plain matrix multiplication:

$$
T =
\begin{bmatrix}
R & \mathbf{t} \\
\mathbf{0}^\top & 1
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
T_{AC} = T_{AB}\,T_{BC}
$$

A point is carried between frames as $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_A = T_{AB}\,\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_B$,
where $\tilde{\mathbf{p}} = \begin{bmatrix} x & y & z & 1 \end{bmatrix}^\top$ is the
homogeneous form. The inverse is:

$$
T^{-1} =
\begin{bmatrix}
R^\top & -R^\top \mathbf{t} \\
\mathbf{0}^\top & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Velocity & the Jacobian

The **Jacobian** $J(\mathbf{q})$ links joint velocities $\dot{\mathbf{q}}$ to
end-effector velocity (linear + angular), and is the workhorse of manipulation
and inverse kinematics:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v} \\ \boldsymbol{\omega} \end{bmatrix} = J(\mathbf{q})\,\dot{\mathbf{q}}
$$

- Resolved-rate control (a practical IK step): $\dot{\mathbf{q}} = J^{+}\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{desired}}$, where $J^{+}$ is the pseudo-inverse.
- **Singularities** are configurations where $J$ loses rank — the arm can't move instantaneously in some direction, and $J^{+}$ blows up.

## Gradients & optimization

Learning and control both minimize a loss $\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$. The
gradient $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{L}$ points uphill, so we step
against it:

$$
\boldsymbol{\theta} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\theta} - \alpha\,\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta})
$$

with learning rate $\alpha$. Everything from PID tuning to training a VLA model is
a variation on "measure error, follow the gradient down."

## Gaussians & state estimation

Sensor noise is modeled as a Gaussian $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)$ with
mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$ and covariance $\Sigma$:

$$
p(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{n}\,|\Sigma|}}\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu})^\top \Sigma^{-1}(\mathbf{x}-\boldsymbol{\mu})\right)
$$

The **Kalman filter** alternates two steps to track a state over time:

- **Predict:** push the state through the motion model; covariance grows.
- **Update:** fold in a measurement; covariance shrinks toward the more certain estimate.

That predict/update rhythm — a belief nudged by motion and corrected by
observation — is the mathematical heart of Part II.

## Symbol cheat sheet

| Symbol | Meaning |
| :----: | ------- |
| $R,\ SO(3)$ | Rotation matrix / group of 3D rotations |
| $T,\ SE(3)$ | Homogeneous transform / group of rigid poses |
| $q$ | Unit quaternion (orientation) |
| $\mathbf{q},\ \dot{\mathbf{q}}$ | Joint positions / velocities |
